## 0.2 – 0.4 — the tarball baselines

Before this repository Syna was delivered as source archives. 0.3.0 introduced the refined package-scoped API and the first executable implementation of the frozen Syna Core Semantic Model v0; 0.4.0 split the core into the definition registry, resolution realms, graph builder, entry planner, materializer, bounded plan cache and implementation directory, added `override()`, private resolution realms, `loadAll()`, `Symbol.asyncDispose`, topology preflight and an adversarial suite of 88 runtime tests (the 0.4.0 entry of `CHANGELOG.md` lists the rest). The 0.4.0 archive, `syna-v0.4.0-source.tar.gz` (sha256 `e0f21a94765aeb9f8e9e7987d596844e4d1bf56fce3584c8de1358131f42a96c`), is the first commit of this repository, `de1d441` (2026-09-04): its changelog with the 0.3.0 and 0.4.0 entries, its READMEs, its own validation record and its benchmark records — `benchmarks/results-v0.3.0.json` and `benchmarks/results-v0.4.0.json`, while `benchmarks/results-v0.4.0-baseline-same-machine.json` is the 0.4.0 workload re-run on the 0.5 machine for `docs/VALIDATION.md`'s v0.4 comparison. Nothing of 0.2 survives here beyond that changelog's reference to the earlier line. An earlier prototype of the idea, an April 2026 context-proxy experiment of eleven commits, is kept on `github.com/synajs/syna` as the branch `legacy/context-proxy` and the tag `archive/2026-04-context-proxy`; it is not a baseline of any release recorded here.

## 1.0.0-rc.5 — the tests that hold（测试有效性）

Task book `work/tasks/SYNA_RC5_EXECUTION_PROMPT.md` (goal `work/tasks/SYNA_RC5_GOAL.txt`). **The first round in which the object under review was the suite, not the code.** A third independent review of 1.0.0-rc.4 (`b691067`) confirmed the closing paths and found no production defect; what it found was that two wrong implementations pass the suite, and it proved it rather than argued it — in an isolated copy of the compiled core it reduced both `joinClose()` methods to `await null; return` (55 of 55 cases still green) and armed every waiter deadline at four times its configured value (8 of 8 still green). Both were reproduced here before anything was changed (`work/rc5/BASELINE.md`), and both are now rejected.

The lesson the round is built on is that neither gap is fixed by a narrower window. An assertion that only says "not resolved" cannot see an observer that was let go early; an upper bound wide enough not to flake under load is wide enough to hold a deadline that is wrong by a factor — and rc.4's own G1 finding was that tightening such a bound to zero tolerance is what made the gate flaky. The criterion has to change: the re-entry matrix asserts what *both* observers are doing while the cleanup is still running, and a controlled clock dates each deadline to the millisecond, with the end-to-end wall-clock check kept and its tolerance stated as what it is. The same principle reaches the reference application, whose acquire deadline is now established by varying it rather than by an upper bound. Three further cases were made to assert what their titles claimed — a diagnostic callback that had been entered zero times, a retention case that ran two of four paths, and matrices that waited for a phase by sleeping — and both counter-examples are kept as a manual audit whose record the gate reads.

With the suite repaired, the round used it as a net for internal quality: `materializer.ts` split into the five concerns it orchestrates (1720 lines to 601 plus five modules, no test changed), the core suites regrouped by the behaviour they hold rather than by the review round that produced them, and the four copied gate scripts replaced by one gate and a profile per release. One item was withdrawn rather than delivered: splitting `descriptors.ts` moves public declarations out of `descriptors.d.ts`, which four tests read by name, so it fails the very criterion it was given — recorded in `docs/SEMANTIC_CHANGES_RC5.md` §3.5. Record: `docs/SEMANTIC_CHANGES_RC5.md`; evidence `validation/v1.0.0-rc.5-release/`, `work/rc5/STATE.md`.

## 1.0.0-rc.4 — the cleanup phase（清理阶段）

Task book `work/tasks/SYNA_RC4_EXECUTION_PROMPT.md` (goal `work/tasks/SYNA_RC4_GOAL.txt`). **The first round driven by a three-party cross-review**: three reviewers independently reproduced the same list in environments rebuilt from the lockfile, on 1.0.0-rc.3 (`9c57269`) — the tree rc.3 had just closed. `work/rc4/ROOT_CAUSE.md` is the root-cause report the round started from and `work/rc4/BASELINE.md` the reproduction baseline, including how each new test reads against the pre-fix source; the probes are in `work/rc4/probes/` and none of them was taken into the test suite as it stands — the tests assert the flipped behaviour.

Two root causes ran through the list. The Runtime executed user code while its own invariants were only half established: `AbortController.abort()` runs its listeners synchronously, and at that moment neither the Promise of the close existed (a listener that re-entered `dispose()` started a second close that raced the first for the same slots, and a cleanup failure could end up in whichever of the two nobody awaited) nor was the close set closed (a listener saw descendants that were still `ready` and could start a dormant Service inside the set being closed, its `setup()` really running, and could push the close past its own time bound). And a cleanup phase had only an overall result, with neither visibility into its progress nor a lifetime of its own: a failure that was already determined disappeared behind a later cleanup of the same phase that hung, an `async` frame suspended on such a cleanup kept `slot` and `owner` in its register file and with them the whole Env graph, and the waiters of an attempt lost their deadline the moment the raw setup Promise settled — so a setup that failed at 10 ms with a cleanup that hung left every `load()`, and `enter()` itself, pending for ever on a perfectly healthy Env. In the reference application `acquireTimeoutMs` still stopped at the door of the site creation. §11 and §13 were clarified, not revised, and the public API inventory is identical to the rc.3 record item by item. The round also split the one gate assertion that had made a cloud run PARTIAL (a zero-tolerance wall-clock lower bound: 39 ms measured against a 40 ms budget) into a deterministic structural half and a wall-clock half with a stated tolerance, and added a fixed-seed property test over random graphs. Record: `docs/SEMANTIC_CHANGES_RC4.md`; evidence `validation/v1.0.0-rc.4-release/`, `work/rc4/STATE.md`.

## 1.0.0-rc.3 — the closing paths（关闭路径）

Task book `work/tasks/SYNA_RC3_EXECUTION_PROMPT.md` (goal `work/tasks/SYNA_RC3_GOAL.txt`). **The first round driven by an independent audit rather than by a task the maintainer set**: a review of 1.0.0-rc.2 (`d7a4410`) reproduced six groups of defects, all of them on the closing path, and the round fixes exactly those. The audit's own package was not in the workspace when the round ran, so its seven probes were reconstructed from the task book's description of each defect and kept in `work/rc3/probes/`; `work/rc3/BASELINE.md` records the seven reproduced on rc.2, the minimal public-surface increment, the scheduling design of the concurrent destruction and the ten strong-reference paths that kept a closed Env alive.

What the audit found and the round fixed: a Ready slot's cleanup was awaited without a bound, so one `onDispose` that never settled stopped the whole close (L1 — the disposal grace now bounds each slot's cleanup phase, independent components are destroyed concurrently, and what outlives its budget is abandoned and reported with the new `attempt-abandoned` phase `'cleanup'`); a cleanup that threw while the Env was closing could reach neither `dispose()` nor an event, and with the waiter cancelled or timed out it was visible nowhere at all (L2 — every cleanup failure the close waited for is in the `AggregateError` exactly once, and the late events are reported from the start of the close); an outstanding attempt kept the closed Env's whole graph through `attempt.slot.ownerEnv` (L3 — an implementation correction: `docs/SEMANTIC_MODEL.md` §13 had said since 0.7 that retention is the user's Promise, never the Runtime's); and in the reference application the owner's abort set the same flag the shutdown used as its "already done" marker, `acquireTimeoutMs` did not cover the configuration read, and a shutdown did not end an in-flight caller's wait (A1, A2/A3). §13 was revised for L1 and L2, and now says in one line what a bounded close is: **what is bounded is the waiting, not the release of resources.** Record: `docs/SEMANTIC_CHANGES_RC3.md`; evidence `validation/v1.0.0-rc.3-release/`, `work/rc3/STATE.md`.
